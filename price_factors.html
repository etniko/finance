Factors Affecting Transaction Price

Analysis of how each factor impacts the purchase price in the Energy / First Buy acquisition scenario.

No Compulsion to Transact

Decrease

Energy has been losing money and may be in jeopardy of going out of business; thus, it may be a motivated vendor. This compulsion weakens the seller's negotiating position, pushing the price down.

Negotiating Abilities

Decrease

First Buy has completed many acquisitions, while Paula may be inexperienced. First Buy may be a better negotiator, which suggests that Paula should have the negotiations performed by an experienced professional.

Restricted Market

Decrease

Energy may be dealing with only one buyer. A restricted market limits competition, removing upward pricing pressure and giving the buyer greater leverage.

Full Knowledge

Decrease or Increase

First Buy is not familiar with the Canadian market (i.e., differing political and economic environments, demographics, and tax legislation). This knowledge gap could cause the buyer to under- or over-value the target.

Informed / Prudent Parties

Increase

First Buy may be very interested in entering the Canadian market. Strong strategic motivation can cause a buyer to pay a premium above fair market value.

Non-Cash Payment Terms

Increase

First Buy may pay some of the purchase price in the form of deferred or conditional consideration, rather than paying cash at closing. Non-cash terms often result in a nominally higher stated price.

No Compulsion to Transact

Decrease

Energy has been losing money and may be in jeopardy of going out of business; thus, it may be a motivated vendor. A motivated seller typically accepts a lower price to ensure the deal closes, weakening their bargaining position in negotiations.

Negotiating Abilities

Decrease

First Buy has completed many acquisitions, while Paula may be inexperienced. First Buy may be a better negotiator. It is recommended that Paula have the negotiations performed by an experienced professional to mitigate this disadvantage.

Restricted Market

Decrease

Energy may be dealing with only one buyer. When the pool of potential acquirers is limited, there is no competitive tension to drive the price upward, giving First Buy significant leverage in the negotiation.

Full Knowledge

Decrease or Increase

First Buy is not familiar with the Canadian market — including differing political and economic environments, demographics, and tax legislation. This knowledge asymmetry creates uncertainty: the buyer may undervalue the target (price decrease) or miscalculate synergies, leading to overpayment (price increase).

Informed / Prudent Parties

Increase

First Buy may be very interested in entering the Canadian market. A buyer with strong strategic intent often assigns a strategic premium to the deal, resulting in a higher purchase price relative to a purely financial buyer with no synergy expectations.

Non-Cash Payment Terms

Increase

First Buy may pay some of the purchase price in the form of deferred or conditional consideration (e.g., earnouts), rather than paying cash at closing. Sellers often demand a higher nominal price in exchange for accepting the additional credit risk and time value of money associated with non-cash terms.

About This Tool


This interactive reference summarizes the key factors that deviate a transaction price from Fair Market Value (FMV) in the context of a business acquisition — specifically the Energy Ltd. / First Buy scenario commonly studied in CBV (Chartered Business Valuator) exam preparation materials.


Each factor is analyzed under the standard FMV premise conditions: willing buyer, willing seller, no compulsion, full knowledge, and arm's-length transaction. Deviations from these conditions create price adjustments above or below FMV.
